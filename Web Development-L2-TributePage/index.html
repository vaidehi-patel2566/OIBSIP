A LIFE OF SCIENCE AND SERVICE

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man of India — scientist, teacher, and 11th President of India.

1931 — 2015 Rameswaram, India

THE SCIENTIST

A tribute

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. He grew up in a modest household, sold newspapers as a student, and carried a lasting curiosity for flight, space and the discipline of science. Those early years shaped a public life built on work, humility and a belief that knowledge should serve the nation.

After studying physics and aerospace engineering, Kalam joined India’s defence and space programmes. At DRDO and later ISRO, he helped turn ambitious missile and satellite projects into working systems. The successful launch of SLV-III in 1980, which placed the Rohini satellite in orbit, became a defining moment for Indian space capability and for his own career as an engineer who could move ideas from paper to launch pad.

He later led work on ballistic missile development, including programmes that earned him the popular title “Missile Man of India”. In 1998 he played a key scientific role during the Pokhran-II nuclear tests. Through these years he remained known less for ceremony than for long hours in laboratories, a notebook full of sketches, and a habit of speaking with students as if they were already colleagues.

From 2002 to 2007 he served as the 11th President of India. The office did not pull him away from teaching. After leaving Rashtrapati Bhavan he returned to lecture halls, wrote widely on dreams, education and national development, and spent his final day, 27 July 2015, addressing students in Shillong. This page remembers a scientist who treated public service as an extension of the classroom.

THE YEARS

Key achievements

  1. 1931

    Born in Rameswaram

    Began life on the island town of Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, in a family of limited means.

  2. 1969

    Joined ISRO

    Moved from defence research into India’s space programme and project leadership.

  3. 1980

    SLV-III success

    India placed the Rohini satellite in orbit, a landmark in self-reliant space technology.

  4. 1998

    Pokhran-II

    Served as a principal scientific advisor during India’s nuclear tests at Pokhran.

  5. 2002–2007

    President of India

    Held the office of the 11th President while remaining a public advocate for education.

  6. 2015

    Final lecture

    Passed away while speaking to students at IIM Shillong, still in the role of a teacher.
